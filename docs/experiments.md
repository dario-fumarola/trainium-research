# The experiments and their controls

## Written calculations and supervised learning

**October 16–22 · Qwen3-1.7B-Base.** The first question was whether a model could generate a useful intermediate calculation from information available before the answer. On 384 fresh real-document arithmetic cases, supervised trace models answered 342 and 335 correctly across two seeds. Matched direct-answer models answered 275 and 285 correctly. The trace models generated about 34 additional actions per case and received more supervised target tokens. Ordinary-text loss worsened relative to the original base. [Arithmetic study and controls](../studies/01-useful-thoughts/docs/EXPERIMENT_HISTORY_20261019.md)

The permutation experiment made the distinction between executing a trace and learning a better direct answerer especially clear. With generated traces the model solved 18,432/18,432 and 18,430/18,432 unseen in-range compositions. Removing the trace largely removed the benefit, and longer compositions remained difficult. On natural document QA, the two-seed study missed its inference and training criteria. Passage-selection, format and coefficient controls are recorded with the original failures. [Permutation results](../studies/01-useful-thoughts/validation/permutation_program_v28_complete_20261020/REPORT.md) · [QA decisions](../studies/01-useful-thoughts/validation/document_QA_v21_complete_20261019.json) · [Evidence-selection studies](../studies/01-useful-thoughts/docs/archive/README_through_20261021.md)

**Would this help a reader on a typical document?** Six frozen selectors chose when to use a calculation. On 1,024 fresh calculation continuations all six improved over the base by 0.421–0.506 nats per 16-token span. Five beat every extra control. On 4,096 unenriched natural windows every selector lost, by 0.020–0.052 nats per span. A constant ensemble without calculations gained 0.0244. Selecting arithmetic cases changes the population enough to change the conclusion. [Fresh reader comparison](../studies/01-useful-thoughts/validation/reader_fresh_confirmation_complete_20261021/REPORT.md)

**Would computing during training improve the weights?** The earlier scaffold experiment missed its direct-prediction criterion, and its baseline reweighted selected continuations. The follow-up trained a uniform next-token baseline. The chosen schedule improved fresh FineMath loss by 0.014481 nats/token (95% interval 0.013903–0.015058), but lost 0.008835 on educational English. Six more fits compared ordinary training, an empty-format auxiliary, and verified computation after the document. All six improved over the starting model. Computation beat format-only and lost to ordinary by 0.000944 and 0.000908 nats/token in the two orders. Independent CPU forwards preserved the direction. [Baseline](../studies/01-useful-thoughts/validation/uniform_lm_baseline_complete_20261022/REPORT.md) · [Six matched fits](../studies/01-useful-thoughts/validation/scaffold_learning_complete_20261022/REPORT.md) · [CPU verification](../studies/01-useful-thoughts/validation/scaffold_standard_CPU_verification_20261022/REPORT.md)

**How we made the six fits comparable.** We crossed two source orders with three objectives. Each fit started from the same released base and saw the same 32,768 ordinary windows plus 2,048 calculation windows. Each 528-token window contributed 527 next-token targets. The raw arm stopped there. On calculation rows the format arm learned an end marker after a supplied header, while the trace arm learned verified steps and the end marker. Putting that suffix after the document kept it out of the context used to predict the document itself. All six fits used 128 AdamW updates, a global batch of 272, eight ranks and 17 accumulation steps. That left the extra supervision as the intended difference between arms. [Packing and labels](../studies/01-useful-thoughts/train/scaffold_learning.py) · [Fixed design](../studies/01-useful-thoughts/configs/scaffold_learning_overnight_v1.json) · [Replay from token scores](../replay/scaffold-learning-20261022/)

**What did improve both domains?** Keeping the ordinary objective and mixing equal shares of FineMath and educational English improved fresh math by 0.011346 and 0.011204 nats/token and English by 0.008550 and 0.008756. The all-math reference still won on math alone. The overnight program reports 56.900 assigned chip-hours across its five accounted studies, including failed reservations. The twelve released models include the weaker controls. [Source mixing](../studies/01-useful-thoughts/validation/uniform_mixture_complete_20261022/REPORT.md) · [Accounting and release](../studies/01-useful-thoughts/docs/OVERNIGHT_RESULTS_20261022.md)

## Early versus late pauses

**October 22 · Randomly initialized Qwen3-0.6B architecture.** The plan inserted 16 unlabeled positions per 128 real tokens, either from the first update or from halfway through training. A third model used ordinary next-token prediction. Five qualification attempts consumed 11.863 assigned chip-hours. Compilation and device occupancy were observed, but the pilot closed before update-and-recovery qualification passed. There is no early-versus-late quality score. [Design](../studies/02-pause-pilot/docs/PAUSE_PRETRAINING_V1.md) · [Closure receipt](../studies/02-pause-pilot/validation/closure_20261022/summary.json)

For the proposed 1,024-update comparison, the late arm would switch at update 512 while keeping its optimizer state. The implementation places the next real token's prediction on the last pause position and assigns zero targets to the inserted positions themselves. The CPU tests exercise that boundary, cached decoding across it and optimizer restoration at the switch. These are implementation checks for the proposed comparison. [Packing code](../studies/02-pause-pilot/train/pause_pretraining.py) · [Boundary and recovery tests](../studies/02-pause-pilot/tests/test_pause_pretraining.py)

**October 22 · Separate 150M natural-text experiment.** This successor did finish. At about one billion real targets, fixed early pauses scored 2.573797 NLL against 2.519907 for ordinary training. Ordinary won in all three paired seeds and every domain. This answers the fixed-pause comparison at that exposure. The 0.6B timing question remains open. [Learning curves and costs](../studies/03-autonomous-compute/results/learning_curve_20261022/REPORT.md)

## Repeated layers and learned workspaces

**October 23–27 · Reusing layers.** A variable-depth model trained to run one, two or three passes improved by 0.027214 nats when evaluated at depth three versus depth one on a frozen test. Yet its two-pass loss was worse than fixed-two training and ordinary training at matched work. Later fixed recurrence beat a one-pass relative, but an eight-layer ordinary control had lower average development loss, 2.480522 versus 2.483862. The two-seed interval crossed zero. With human-written arithmetic steps progressively withheld, a recurrent model solved 4/135 held-out answers despite over 99% accuracy on a training sample. The team stopped the recurrence campaign on October 27. [Variable-depth test](../studies/03-autonomous-compute/results/variable_depth_20261023/test/REPORT.md) · [Eight-layer control](../studies/03-autonomous-compute/results/lean_baseline_controls_20261026/main/ordinary8/REPORT.md) · [Arithmetic curriculum](../studies/03-autonomous-compute/results/natural_chain_continuation_pilot_20261025/recovery-v2/REPORT.md) · [Change of research direction](../studies/03-autonomous-compute/docs/WORKSPACE_RESEARCH_CHARTER_20261027.md)

**October 27–29 · Learning what to write.** A pretrained 1.485B model generated temporary workspaces and received a predictive training signal. On 320 development anchors, ordinary continuation scored 2.387626 next-four-token NLL, versus 2.422095 for the learned writer. Giving the reader the actual future as a privileged diagnostic helped substantially. The writer could not supply an equally useful signal from the prefix. A continuous-workspace variant also trailed ordinary, by 0.004209 nats on its full-window measure. Residual-memory, probability-refinement and sparse-workspace controls tested whether the content or an extra pass accounted for gains. Replacing memory often retained most of the benefit, and the sparse model lost its matched-work comparison. ## Persistent internal states

**October 29–30 · Training from scratch.** Here the model generated a continuous vector after each real token. Later tokens could attend to it. At 128M targets, a selected seed beat the strongest tuned ordinary control by 0.025047 nats on reused development data. Two new seeds gained 0.002267 and 0.028967, missing the prespecified replication criterion. Varying the number of internal steps also failed to produce a monotonic improvement. [Tuned comparison](../studies/03-autonomous-compute/results/interleaved_workspace_20261029/tied-comparison-v1/REPORT.md) · [Replication](../studies/03-autonomous-compute/results/interleaved_workspace_20261029/seed-replication-v1/REPORT.md) · [Step budget](../studies/03-autonomous-compute/results/budgeted_interleaved_20261029/learning-v1/REPORT.md)

With 1B targets per model, the latent model improved broad loss by 0.030495 nats. Removing access to its own past states raised broad loss by 0.636840. That intervention establishes dependence on the trained pathway. On the 259 source-audited arithmetic cases it correctly predicted only five answer prefixes. At matched nominal matrix work, ordinary training saw 3.783B targets and won broad loss by 0.110176 nats and arithmetic answer loss by 0.088747. Conditional intervals describe source groups for these checkpoints. Training-seed variation remains a separate uncertainty. [Equal exposure](../studies/03-autonomous-compute/results/latent_exposure_20261030/learning-v1/REPORT.md) · [State ablations](../studies/03-autonomous-compute/results/latent_exposure_20261030/content-recovery-v1/REPORT.md) · [Matched work](../studies/03-autonomous-compute/results/latent_exposure_20261030/work-recovery-v1/REPORT.md)

**How we tested the one-billion-target result.** We started all arms from random initialization at seed 730271. The generated-state and constant-pause models each had 51,747,328 parameters. The ordinary control had 11 layers, tied embeddings and 51,179,008 parameters. Each received 15,287 updates on the same indexed source order, using AdamW, a 0.001 learning rate, 64 warmup updates and a final 10% cooldown. Only after the equal-data comparison met its thresholds did we train the ordinary control for 57,822 updates to match matrix work. We scored 384 broad development windows and 259 arithmetic expressions. We had excluded 94 expressions found in the longer training prefix before fitting. Arithmetic answer loss weights the three operation-count bins equally. [Frozen recipe and scoring rules](../studies/03-autonomous-compute/docs/LATENT_LEARNING_EXPOSURE_20261030.md) · [Exact sequential scoring implementation](../studies/03-autonomous-compute/compute_trainium/interleaved_sequential_reference.py) · [Matched-work execution record](../studies/03-autonomous-compute/results/latent_exposure_20261030/work-recovery-v1/report.json)

**Engineering result.** Batching gradient reduction and clipping cut a warm ordinary-model update from 0.2934 to 0.0616 seconds. Reversing the order gave 0.2884 and 0.0612 seconds. The qualification checked gradients, optimizer moments, agreement across 64 replicas and a saved next-update replay. The 4.71–4.77× speedup applies to this ordinary training graph.
